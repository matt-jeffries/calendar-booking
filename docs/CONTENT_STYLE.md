# Content style

Use clear operational language: plain-spoken, confident and practical British English.

Say what it is, what it does and why it helps—in that order.

## Principles

- **Direct:** say exactly what the person can do.
- **Concise:** keep one clear idea in each sentence.
- **Outcome-led:** describe the result, not the technical process.
- **Human:** sound natural without becoming promotional or overly polished.
- **Confident:** state what happens without hype or unnecessary hedging.
- **Specific:** name the meeting, available time, invitation or action involved.
- **Respectful:** assume people know why they want the meeting.
- **Consistent:** use the same term for the same thing throughout the product.
- **British:** use UK spelling and an understated tone.

## Product terms

Use these terms consistently:

- **meeting** for the calendar event
- **available time** for a time someone can choose
- **calendar invitation** for the invitation sent to the attendee
- **booking link** for a private management URL
- **reschedule** and **cancel** for meeting changes

Avoid switching between booking, appointment, event and session when referring to the meeting itself.

## Examples

| Avoid | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Your time is reserved. | Your meeting is booked. |
| Let’s find a time that works. | Choose an available time. |
| Nearly there. | Confirm your meeting. |
| Calendar integration unavailable. | We couldn’t check the calendars. Try again. |
| Continue securely. | Connect calendar. |

## Interface checklist

Before adding or changing text, check that it:

1. names the action or outcome;
2. uses the established product terms;
3. removes technical detail the person cannot act on;
4. tells the person what to do next when something fails;
5. uses British spelling and punctuation.
